# Dev Diary --- August 28, 2026

## Code Refinement and Fieldwork Coordination

Today was a relatively light development day. I spent some time going back over the machine learning code I had developed earlier in the week and polishing it. The main focus was cleaning up and refining the implementation after the recent work on material classification and the UV-based processing pipeline. No major new functionality was introduced today, but this gave me an opportunity to consolidate the work from earlier in the week and leave the code in a cleaner state for continued development and eventual use in the final project pipeline.

I also made further progress toward arranging real-world testing locations for BEHOLDER.

I received a positive response from **Scenic Caves Nature Adventures** regarding my request to conduct a scan at their site. They offered complimentary admission for the research visit and asked whether I would be able to provide photographs or other materials produced during the project for their use afterwards. They indicated that the summer period is currently too busy to comfortably accommodate the work, but that they would be open to a weekday visit after Labour Day.

I replied confirming that this timing would work well. I also offered to provide Scenic Caves with photographs and other useful material produced during the visit and, assuming the photogrammetric reconstruction is successful, a copy of the completed digital 3D model. Since I am attempting to coordinate several field locations within the same trip, I asked whether they have a preferred Monday or Tuesday during September that I could plan the other visits around.

I also contacted **Parks Canada**, specifically **Tricia Robins and Cavan Harpur**, regarding the possibility of conducting a scan at **the Grotto in Bruce Peninsula National Park**. This would potentially provide another excellent cave environment for testing the system. However, I am considerably less confident that this location will be possible within the project timeline because Parks Canada normally requires approximately **90 days** to process research permit applications. I contacted them regardless to determine whether there are any options available given the limited scope and academic nature of the project.

Finally, I continued investigating **general liability insurance** for the proposed Bruce Caves Conservation Area fieldwork. Obtaining useful quotes has been more difficult than expected. Several insurance providers have poorly designed online systems for this type of unusual short-term research activity, and finding appropriate direct contacts has also been challenging. I have not yet received a usable quote, so this remains an unresolved logistical requirement for the Bruce Caves visit.

Overall, today involved relatively little new implementation work, but it helped consolidate the machine learning development completed earlier in the week while continuing to move the field-testing portion of the project forward. Scenic Caves in particular now appears to be a promising additional location for testing BEHOLDER in a real cave environment.
